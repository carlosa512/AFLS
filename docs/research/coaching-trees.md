# Coaching trees in the NFL

Calibration for `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-28-personality-relationships-design.md` §4. Gathered 2026-10-28
from web searches. Several pages (si.com, theringer.com, fivethirtyeight.com) could not be opened from the research
session, so their figures come from search summaries rather than the pages themselves. Lines marked **(est.)** are
our own counts or judgments, not sourced figures.

## How common trees are

- **The press counts loosely.** In 2018 ESPN tied 28 of 32 head coaches to Parcells or Belichick; in 1998 half the
  league traced to Walsh or Landry ([Wikipedia: Coaching tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaching_tree)). In 2007,
  14 of 32 were counted as Walsh descendants
  ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/bayarea/article/Bill-Walsh-planted-the-ultimate-coaching-tree-2549658.php)).
- **A strict count is much smaller.** Counting direct assistants only:
  - Shanahan/McVay: 11 head coaches in 2026, the two roots likely included
    ([RealGM](https://football.realgm.com/wiretap/54174/Shanahan-McVay-Tree-Now-Accounts-For-11-NFL-Head-Coaches)).
  - Reid: 4 active in 2024, 5 in 2022–23
    ([pro-football-history.com](https://pro-football-history.com/blog/10/98/current-nfl-coaching-trees)).
  - Carroll: 2 active in 2025 (Quinn, Canales)
    ([Raiders](https://www.raiders.com/news/pete-carroll-coaching-tree-dan-quinn-dave-canales-brian-schottenheimer)).
- **(est.)** In 2025, four or five trees had two or more active head coaches. Before 2017 one dominant tree at a time
  was typical.

## How long a link takes

- The usual convention counts **one season** on a staff, and short stops are counted routinely. Brandon Staley's one
  year as the Rams' DC puts him in McVay's tree ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Staley)); Matt
  LaFleur's single year as McVay's OC does the same.
- Long links exist too: Mike LaFleur spent four seasons under Kyle Shanahan and three under McVay
  ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/nfl-coaching-hires-grades-report-cards-2026-cycle/)); Raheem Morris
  spent three as the Rams' DC.
- Eight of Andy Reid's eleven branches came off his first Eagles staff
  ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/kansas-city-chiefs/post/_/id/26030/how-andy-reid-grew-the-nfls-most-prolific-coaching-tree)).

**For AFLS:** the owner chose a stricter rule (3 seasons together) so that trees are rare. AFLS trees will therefore
be smaller than the press's.

## Hiring

- 53 head coaches were hired in 2017–2024; 23 of the 33 offensive hires had called plays
  ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2025/02/04/nfl/nfl-play-calling-network-andy-reid-sean-mcvay-kyle-shanahan-hiring-cycle)).
  No source counts hires by tree.
- **(est.)** From hire lists: Shanahan/McVay branches took about 2 of 7–9 hires in 2017, 2019, 2021 and 2022, none in
  2018 or 2020, and 1–2 in 2023–25: about one hire in five. The 2026 cycle was a peak at 4 of 10
  ([Sharp Football](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/analysis/nfl-coaching-changes-firings-hirings-rumors-2026/)).
- McVay saw 7 assistants become head coaches during his own tenure (from 2017)
  ([FantasyNerds](https://fantasynerds.com/news/story/2026/09/08/sean-mcvays-coaching-tree-continues-to-grow-producing-nfl-head-coaches-1617221)).
  **(est.)** His first two came about two years after he was hired.

## Rise and decline

- **Belichick, a withering tree.** His disciples were a combined 208-296-1 (.413) by November 2020
  ([NBC Sports Boston](https://www.nbcsportsboston.com/nfl/new-england-patriots/patricias-firing-highlights-failures-of-belichick-coaching-tree/220616/)):
  Crennel 32-63, McDaniels 20-33, Mayo 4-13 and fired after one season
  ([NESN](https://nesn.com/2025/01/patriots-reportedly-fire-jerod-mayo-after-one-season)). **(est.)** The first wave
  (hired 2005–06) was gone within three or four years; a second wave (2018–22) also failed.
- **Walsh, a long tail.** McVay and LaFleur still traced to him in 2019
  ([NFL.com](https://www.nfl.com/news/even-nfl-s-newest-coaches-branch-from-parcells-walsh-trees-0ap3000001009347)),
  about thirty years after he last coached **(est.)**.
- **Dungy, a fade.** His tree's record is 470-448-5
  ([pro-football-history.com](https://pro-football-history.com/coach/108/tony-dungy-bio)); its last active branch,
  Tomlin, left in January 2026
  ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/mike-mccarthy-hired-pittsburgh-steelers-coach-mike-tomlin/)).
- **Holmgren** had 8 branches ([coaching-tree.app](https://coaching-tree.app/mike-holmgren)); the line lives on
  mainly through Reid.

## Generations

- Reid had 11 branches by 2025
  ([Yahoo](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/andy-reid-coaching-tree-came-113000474.html)), 7 of them active at once in 2018.
- Walsh → Holmgren → Reid → Harbaugh, Pederson, Nagy, McDermott, Sirianni is four generations. Shanahan → McVay →
  Taylor, O'Connell is similar.
- **(est.)** Few disciples found a tree of their own: about one in five to eight (Kyle Shanahan and McVay did;
  Pederson and Nagy haven't).

## How disciples do

- McVay's four active disciples: 184-132-2 (.582), 11 playoff trips in 19 seasons (SI, via search summary).
- Reid's 11 branches average .550 with 23 playoff wins; Belichick's 9 average .42 with 2
  ([pro-football-history.com](https://pro-football-history.com/blog/10/13/success-of-modern-coaching-trees)).
- First-time head coaches overall: .447, 41.7% reach the playoffs
  ([pro-football-history.com](https://pro-football-history.com/blog/10/121/breaking-down-the-success-of-firsttime-nfl-head-coaches)).
- 20 head coaches were let go within three years of being hired over the last five years; current head coaches
  average 3.9 years in the job
  ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/mikestraw/2026/01/20/a-third-of-nfl-head-coaches-are-fired-within-two-years-why-it-matters/)).

## What AFLS takes from this (est.)

- A hot tree takes about 20–25% of a hiring cycle; a cold one close to none.
- A hot tree's disciples win about .55–.58, roughly 10 points above first-time hires (.45). A withering tree's win
  about .41–.42.
- A tree can stay alive 20–30 years through its branches after its root retires.
- About one disciple in five to eight who becomes a head coach starts a tree of his own.
