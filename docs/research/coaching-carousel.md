# The coaching carousel: step-downs and refusals

Calibration for `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-30-coach-character-design.md` §2 (agency). Gathered 2026-10-30 from
web searches. No source counts either figure below, so the targets are our own reading of the examples. Lines marked
**(est.)** are hand counts or judgments, not sourced figures.

## Fired head coaches who coordinate again

Recent examples of former head coaches taking coordinator jobs, most of them within a cycle or two of being let go
([FOX Sports 2025 tracker](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nfl/2025-nfl-coaching-gm-tracker-interviews-rumors-personnel-changes),
[FOX Sports 2026 tracker](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nfl/2026-nfl-coaching-gm-tracker-interviews-rumors-personnel-changes),
[NFL.com tracker](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-coaching-gm-tracker-latest-news-interviews-developments-2026-hiring-cycle)):

- Dennis Allen (Saints) became the Bears' defensive coordinator.
- Brandon Staley (Chargers) became the Saints' defensive coordinator.
- Raheem Morris (Falcons) became the 49ers' defensive coordinator.
- Brian Daboll (Giants) became the Titans' offensive coordinator.
- Mike McDaniel (Dolphins) and Frank Reich (Panthers) were hired as offensive coordinators.
- Gus Bradley (Jaguars) came back as a defensive coordinator years after his firing.

Others say no to the step down. Rex Ryan wanted only head-coaching jobs
([FanSided](https://fansided.com/2014/12/29/rex-ryan-not-interested-defensive-coordinator/)). Ron Rivera said he'd be
open to coordinating ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/ron-rivera-still-wants-coach-230006208.html)). Chuck Pagano took a
coordinator's job and said he didn't want to be a head coach again ("I've done it",
[theScore](https://www.thescore.com/nfl/news/1704715/sms:)).

- **(est.)** Of the head coaches let go in a cycle (about 6–8), two or three are coordinators within two seasons, one
  or two get another head-coach job, and the rest leave the league (TV, college, retirement).

## Coordinators who turn down head-coach talks

Coordinators withdraw from head-coach searches most cycles, usually to stay where they are or after a new deal:

- Dan Quinn withdrew from several searches in one cycle to stay in Dallas
  ([SI](https://www.si.com/nfl/colts/news/colts-head-coach-dan-quinn-withdraws-consideration),
  [Sportsnaut](https://sportsnaut.com/nfl/cowboys-dc-dan-quinn-commits-to-stay-for-22-season)).
- Liam Coen withdrew from Jacksonville's search in January 2025
  ([St. Pete Catalyst](https://stpetecatalyst.com/zaps/bucs-coordinator-will-not-take-head-coaching-job)).
- Grant Udinski withdrew from Cleveland's search after a new deal in Jacksonville
  ([PFT](https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/grant-udinski-withdraws-from-browns-head-coaching-search)).
- Josh McDaniels withdrew from Cleveland's search more than once
  ([National Football Post](https://www.nationalfootballpost.com/2026/05/29/josh-mcdaniels-withdraws-name-for-browns-job),
  [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/josh-mcdaniels-withdraws-himself-from-consideration-for-49ers-opening)).
- Brian Schottenheimer pulled out of Miami's search in 2007 ("I am committed to the New York Jets"), and Teryl Austin
  out of Denver's ([CBS Detroit](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/detroit/news/report-teryl-austin-will-not-meet-with-broncos-as-previously-scheduled)).
- Long careers as coordinators without a head-coach job are common too: Dick LeBeau, Dean Pees, Wade Phillips late in his
  career **(est.)**.

## What AFLS takes from this (est.)

- **Step-downs:** about a third of fired head coaches are coordinators again within two seasons; about one in five
  gets another head-coach job (the retread share, `npm run offseason`: NFL 32% of hires).
- **Refusals:** one to three a cycle where a club's top choice says no, more often a team's own coordinator staying
  put than a stranger.
- **Lifers:** a minority of coordinators, perhaps one in six, never take head-coach talks.
