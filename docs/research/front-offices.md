# Front offices: how real GMs behave (NFL Blue Book)

Source material for `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-05-gm-habits-design.md` (GM archetypes and habits). Gathered
2026-11-05 from Charles Robinson's NFL Blue Book (nflbluebook.com), a Substack on how NFL front offices work. Every
post read was free and complete. Thirteen posts from the 2026 season, listed at the end.

**How to read this note.** It is one reporter's reporting in one half-season. The game takes it as **story fuel**:
habits a GM could plausibly have, not rates to hit. Fun and low micromanagement come first, the NFL is the
foundation, and departures are labeled (the same rule as `docs/PRACTICE_SQUADS.md`). Everything below is
paraphrased; quotes are a few words at most. Real names stay in this note: the game's GMs are fictional.

## 1. QB plans: bridges, auditions and extra quarterbacks

- **Pittsburgh** (Omar Khan) kept four QBs on the 53 in 2026: a 1-year veteran (Aaron Rodgers) plus three young ones.
  Robinson reads the season as an audition to learn whether a young QB can start in 2027, and calls a 10–7 year that
  hides them a bad outcome. Pittsburgh has cycled through veteran stopgaps since Roethlisberger.
- **Cleveland** (Andrew Berry) built young skill players first and holds two 2027 firsts that could go to a QB.
- **Las Vegas** paired a veteran (Kirk Cousins) with a rookie (Fernando Mendoza): the bridge model.
- **Carolina** benched Bryce Young after 18 games, then backed him with guards and a run game; he won the division in
  2025. Robinson argues Tennessee should give Cam Ward the same patience, with a plan behind it.
- **Minnesota** planned a slow path for J.J. McCarthy (third string, then backup), sped it up after a good camp, and
  traded him for a fifth two years later. Robinson spreads the blame across the organization.

**For AFLS:** *Signs a Bridge QB* (a one-year veteran beside a young starter) and *Sticks With His Young QB* (won't
cut or trade a young QB he drafted). Draft and Develop gets the long leash.

## 2. Moving up for a quarterback

- When the 2027 QB class went from five likely first-rounders to one or two, executives expected more teams to trade
  up or reach for one. Scarcity pushes second-round grades into round one.
- Atlanta's GM (Ian Cunningham) was in Chicago when it traded out of No. 1 in 2023 for picks and took its QB a year
  later: a GM can hold or trade down when no QB is clearly special.

**For AFLS:** *Trades Up for a QB*. A habit that read the strength of *next* year's class ("saves for a class") was
dropped: the game builds a draft class when the new league year opens, so in season there is nothing to read.

## 3. When to extend: early, on time or late

- **Philadelphia** (Howie Roseman) gets ahead of the contract market more than any other GM, per Robinson, and traded
  a starter to fund the next extension.
- **Detroit** has extended most of its core early (Sewell, St. Brown, Hutchinson and others). Chicago and Seattle
  extended after year three.
- **Cincinnati** doesn't often move quickly on extensions.
- **Tampa Bay** paid its QB (Baker Mayfield) the top of his expected market without looking desperate.

**For AFLS:** *Extends Early* (two years left), and three archetypes: Market Mover (Roseman-style), Core Keeper
(Detroit-style), Slow Mover (Cincinnati-style). Today's AI never extends before a deal's final year: this habit adds
that pass.

## 4. Selling before paying

- Detroit's GM (Brad Holmes) traded an injury-prone tight end (T.J. Hockenson) rather than extend him in 2022, citing
  future contracts. Robinson thinks Sam LaPorta's stalled extension could go the same way, though a contender rarely
  sells in a playoff push.

**For AFLS:** *Sells a Year Early* (shops starters near a second contract, or with high injury risk). Core Keeper
starts with it.

## 5. Paying firsts for a star

- Indianapolis (Chris Ballard) traded two firsts for a cornerback (Sauce Gardner) and paid a veteran QB coming off an
  Achilles injury; both were listed among the moves that cost him his job.
- Dallas (Jerry Jones) said in July it would lean toward today over three years from now, then made two trades
  (a tackle and a corner) and left the door open for more.
- Selling AFC clubs supplied NFC contenders at the 2026 deadline; Robinson credits front-office relationships.

**For AFLS:** *Trades Firsts for Stars*, the All-In GM's signature.

## 6. Familiarity and reunions

- Kansas City weighed a reunion with Tyreek Hill, who spent six seasons there. Buyers aim at players their staff
  already knows: a former teammate of the QB, a position coach from the player's old club.
- Executives warned against selling a player to a coaching tree that had already judged him poorly: word spreads.

**For AFLS:** *Brings His Guys Back* (values players he drafted).

## 7. Swaps and rentals

- Tennessee and the Jets swapped two young defenders who needed new schemes, one for one. A GM suggested a 1-for-1
  for Marvin Harrison Jr. A rental for a rental is easier to agree than picks for a rental.

**For AFLS:** *Prefers Player Swaps*.

## 8. Best player or need

- San Francisco took a fourth-round defensive tackle its staff judged the best overall player on the board over
  other needs.

**For AFLS:** *Drafts Best Available*.

## 9. The franchise tag as a pause

- Detroit can tag LaPorta and settle it in the spring; tags appear throughout as a way to delay a decision.

**For AFLS:** *Tags Before He Pays*. Hard-Liner and Slow Mover start with it.

## 10. Burned once

- Miami gave Tua Tagovailoa $212M over four years, then released him after paying about $100M. Robinson uses it as
  the warning for Washington's extension of Jayden Daniels after a second elbow injury.
- San Francisco's money spent on Brandon Aiyuk is cited as a reason it may decline Ricky Pearsall's option.

**For AFLS:** *Gun-Shy on Big [Position] Deals*, formed only by a deal of his ending in heavy dead money.

## 11. Can-kicking, and owners (noted, not built)

- Baltimore (Eric DeCosta) restructured Lamar Jackson's 2026 cap hit, pushing an $84M charge into 2027 after the owner
  wanted an extension done; the GM is "on the hook".
- Indianapolis's new owner (Carlie Irsay-Gordon) fired Ballard at 2–4, holding him to the championship standard he
  set at his first press conference. Her father's confidence had bought him years of patience.

**Not in this pass:** the owner blaming a Can-Kicker when the bill lands, and who has the final say at each club,
belong to a later "who runs the club" feature (they would move how often GMs are fired).

## 12. How the league talks about a GM

- Peers judge a GM by his record of moves: before Ballard's firing, people around the league had begun asking why he
  was still getting the chance. Executives polled by Robinson describe each other by habit ("pragmatic and reserved").
- Front offices value players two ways: their own pre-draft grade and college tape, or what the player has shown in
  the NFL.

**For AFLS:** a GM's reputation is what the league says about what he has done. Clues come from his public moves and
lag behind him when he changes.
