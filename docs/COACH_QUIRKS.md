## Pools

There are **six types per role**, not six quirks on every person. Position coaches can become coordinators,
and coordinators can become head coaches. They keep held habits, including inactive ones.

| Role | Quirks |
|---|---|
| Head coach | Clings to Timeouts; Stops the Clock Early; Takes the Points; Backs His Offense; Goes for Two; Distrusts the Long Kick |
| Offensive coordinator | Settles for Three; Shot-Play Architect; Goes Into a Shell; Scripted Opening; Attacks the End Zone; Up-Tempo |
| Defensive coordinator | Third-Down Heat; Trusts His Coverage; Crowds the Line; Protects Against the Deep Ball; Plays Prevent; Disguises Coverages |
| Position coach | Earn Your Reps; Trusts His Veterans; Patient Teacher; Technique Stickler; Keeps Them Fresh; Runs Hard Practices |

**Rarity.** Each habit is a minority's trait. Style habits put two marbles in a bag, not three, so each one
sits in roughly 13–28% of its chairs. Settles for Three was in 44% of offensive coordinators' chairs on three
marbles, and it pushed the league's field-goal attempts past the 2.0 ceiling. Plays Prevent and the common good
habits carry two as well. Disguises Coverages is quality-biased like Shot-Play Architect but one marble rarer
(about one defensive coordinator in ten). A test holds every habit under three chairs in ten across four leagues.

**Opposites.** Settles for Three and Attacks the End Zone, and Keeps Them Fresh and Runs Hard Practices, pull
against each other: a coach who holds one never forms the other.

Medical, scouting, strength and special-teams coaching pools are not included in this pass.

For the NFL evidence behind the shot-design distribution and its current tuning, see
[Coach scheme calibration](research/coach-scheme-calibration.md). The habit identifies a broader good-schemer
tier, not just a handful of exceptional-coach equivalents. Formation uses a soft quality bias with no hard
ratings cutoff; the two-shot game budget is unchanged.

## Grip, bags and career history

`engine/coachQuirks.ts` owns the registry, generation, role eligibility and progression. Each coach has a seeded
annual bag, drawn without replacement. The bag contains habit marbles, fix marbles, and blanks.

- Grip 1: forming, without an established effect.
- Grip 2: established, eligible to apply.
- Grip 3: ingrained; only half of fix attempts stick.
- At most two role-active habits and four held habits. Inactive-role habits do not displace applicable habits.
- Two annual draws; a 10% weekly draw chance for a coach whose team actually played, playoffs included.
- New coaches receive a deterministic, bounded prehistory. No retrospective game incidents or mail are invented.
- Relevant choices provide bounded evidence for the next refill. Timeout mistakes can supply correction marbles.
- Position-room evidence includes close selections and actual position learners. Technique interventions use the
  player's existing fix-marble mechanism; they never remove a playing habit immediately.
- The change log retains twelve dated transitions, including previous grip, formation, gain, weakening and
  complete loss. Lost habits remain in the history. Old entries without previous grip use neutral wording.
  A deeply ingrained habit may last a career but is not hard-locked.

Progression is guarded by season/week stamps. Promotions reconcile newly relevant bag entries once, without
refilling consumed marbles. Hiring does not clear these guards. Coach-specific random streams leave unrelated
generation and market rolls alone.

## Football effects and bounds

Only the actual chair holder supplies the corresponding effect. Assistant habits cannot secretly control the
coordinator's play calls. The match snapshots staff at kickoff, so changes outside a live game do not rewrite it.

- **Clings to Timeouts:** on an otherwise appropriate defensive timeout in Q4, trailing by at most sixteen,
  with 80–180 seconds remaining, a 20% chance to defer through another snap. At most once per game. Mandatory
  endgame and overtime behavior retains priority.
- **Stops the Clock Early:** starts defensive clock stops at 210 rather than 180 seconds in qualifying games.
- **Takes the Points / Backs His Offense:** a 5% chance to shade a short goal-line fourth-down judgment toward
  the kick or attempt respectively. Normal-tempo decisions only, outside late forced decisions.
- **Settles for Three:** early downs in comfortable FG range in a one-score game: pass probability −0.05 and a
  small chance to prefer a short throw over an ordinary deep call.
- **Goes Into a Shell:** with a narrow fourth-quarter lead and more than three minutes left, a similar safer
  call preference. The two caution adjustments together cannot exceed −0.10 pass probability.
- **Goes for Two:** follows the analytics chart into a two-point try where the default call kicks: down eight
  after the touchdown in the second half, and the fourth quarter's chart (−2, +1, +5) in the third. An 80%
  chance in those spots.
- **Distrusts the Long Kick:** on an ordinary fourth down where the default call tries a field goal his kicker
  makes less than three times in four, a 60% chance to punt instead (or go for it on fourth and two or less).
  He trusts a kicker with a field-goal skill of 14 or more, or a Boomer Leg, and kicks as usual. Never on a
  forced end-of-half or late-game kick.
- **Shot-Play Architect:** eligible ordinary early downs from the offense's 25 to 65, at least three yards to
  gain: a 5.5% chance to commit a designed deep pass, up to twice per game. A thrown deep pass receives +0.5
  separation contest points. Pressure, sacks, scrambles, inaccurate throws and coverage can waste the call.
  A committed shot consumes its budget even if no pass reaches a receiver. A canceled pre-snap play does not.
- **Scripted Opening:** +0.25 separation on eligible passes in the first six offensive snaps.
- **Attacks the End Zone:** ordinary early downs from the 20 to the 4: pass probability +0.03 and a 12% chance
  to turn an ordinary short throw into an intermediate one. Lighter than Settles for Three, on purpose.
- **Up-Tempo:** a quicker pace between snaps (18–26 seconds, against a normal 28–38 and a hurry-up's 8–16)
  when trailing in the fourth quarter, down nine or more late in the third, and on any drive in the last two
  and a half minutes of the first half. A full hurry-up on those snaps was worth over a point a game to both
  sides, so it is a quicker tempo, not the two-minute drill.
- **Third-Down Heat / Trusts His Coverage:** small changes to third-and-long pressure choices; existing blitz
  protection and coverage tradeoffs resolve the outcome. No sack/completion bonuses.
- **Crowds the Line:** small run-defense call preference on ordinary early short-yardage downs.
- **Protects Against the Deep Ball:** protecting a late one-score lead, deep separation −0.3 and short +0.15.
- **Plays Prevent:** leading by 9–16 in the last four minutes, a 60% chance each snap to sit in a soft shell:
  coverage call, a three-man rush (pressure −0.8), deep separation −0.5, intermediate +0.4 and short +0.8.
  It rarely loses the game, which is the frustration: it gives up quick yards when the lead should be safe.
- **Disguises Coverages:** on obvious passing downs (third and seven or more, second and ten or more), pressure
  +0.6 contest points and interception odds ×1.15. Quality-biased and rare.

Position-room habits change bodies, not play calls:

- **Keeps Them Fresh:** his room's practice injury odds ×0.88 and three points more of lost condition back
  each week (`roomCare`, read by `injuryWeek`).
- **Runs Hard Practices:** +0.02 development fit for everyone in his room (`coachPlayerFit`) and practice
  injury odds ×1.12.

These are initial tuning values. `npm run coach-quirks -- 100` runs matched fixed-roster experiments for each
game-day habit separately. It reports opportunities, actual uses, stat deltas and changed winners. It does not
declare a quirk balanced because a small sample happens to remain inside a band.

## Fit, development and selection

Work ethic and temperament remain unchanged. Earn Your Reps is a better fit for hard workers and a poorer fit
for coasters, with an additional small mismatch when low work ethic and a short fuse coexist. This is a
coaching-effectiveness rule, not a new universal "bad character" attribute or a guaranteed angry response.

Patient Teacher favors young learners; Trusts His Veterans favors experienced players. Fit is bounded to ±5%
of the existing growth/learning rate, and half that magnitude on technical/mental decline, within existing
staff caps. Physical decline and the age at which decline starts are unchanged.

`coachingForPlayer()` is the per-player input. Annual development targets remain fixed once rolled. A midseason
move changes the staff around him but does not reroll that year's target; the new fit feeds his next annual
roll, and current position-learning work uses its destination room. This permits different career paths under
different staffs without creating transfer-based development farming.

Selection preferences add at most ±0.2 to an internal positional comparison, so they cannot overturn a gap of
more than 0.4 on the 1–20 scale. Automatic charts use the preference; manual charts remain untouched. Existing
OL slot ordering, positional eligibility, injury availability and out-of-position penalties remain in force.

AI and user staffs apply the same football effects. Hiring prices and AI market selection still use the existing
ratings, budget and head-coach vision rules; this pass does not add a separate market value for a coaching quirk.

Conversion preferences rank existing supported motives. They do not bypass body gates, roster needs, learning
slots, cooldowns or a negative verdict. The proposal can name a real teaching advantage or disadvantage; no
random staff argument is generated.

## Former players

The existing probability of entering coaching is unchanged. After entry, selected established playing habits
can seed one analogous coaching habit, usually forming and occasionally established:

- Gunslinger or Manipulates Safety Eyes → Shot-Play Architect.
- Stunt Communicator → Technique Stickler.
- Loose Carriage → occasionally Technique Stickler, reflecting correction of his old weakness.

Inheritance is weighted, not guaranteed. Its provenance stays on the career card. A QB coach's inherited OC
habit remains inactive until he holds that job. The same coach retains it on later promotion.

## Visible consequences

- Balanced tendencies use the same pills as other play-calling styles. Coach habits reuse player quirk
  visual states: dashed while forming, solid when established, and grey when inactive/dormant. Grip and
  role details appear on hover or keyboard focus rather than in the visible pill label.
- A dated "Habits over time" list records formation, gain, weakening and complete loss, alongside playing-career provenance.
- A game recap adds at most one meaningful coaching observation. The HC says "I" about his own decision and
  names the responsible coordinator/position coach when discussing someone else. Poor-performance reactions
  use actual recorded interceptions, fumbles, sacks allowed or catches/targets, not imaginary practice scenes.
  Designed-shot mentions require a recorded 45+ yard completion in a competitive game, or a 20+ yard
  completion on a late go-ahead scoring drive. The accepted play and possession boundary must be present
  in the replay log; missing context never becomes an inferred highlight. Routine shots stay in the film.
- An opposition report can carry a named room coach's close-call selection recommendation in an indented staff note.
- Follow-ups report actual appearances and production. Role-specific opportunity and production checks
  suggest keeping the trial, reconsidering the veteran, or collecting more evidence. They never declare
  a permanent starter or alter the depth chart. Missing opportunities keep the wording cautious.
- Each team retains at most six recommendation threads. An initial note and its follow-up each publish once,
  using the existing inbox message and roster/player actions. No routine standalone coaching mail is added.
- Season memory separately retains discussed players and unordered pairs across history trimming and coach
  changes. It allows at most six initial threads per club/season and spaces new cases in a position by six weeks.
- One later slump revisit is possible only after an adopted, promising trial. The original follow-up must have
  actually published at least four weeks earlier. The last three team games after the trial must each contain
  the player and poor opportunity-bearing production: receivers at least 12 combined targets (3+ each), backs
  24 carries (6+ each), quarterbacks 60 attempts (15+ each). Low volume, a missed game, one good game,
  a reversed chart, unavailable personnel or a departed coach prevents the revisit. The original pair remains
  closed after a rebound. Optional publication stamps survive deleted/pruned messages and save/load.
- DC recaps use bounded post-penalty snapshots of actual calls on late one-score stops, takeaways and conceded
  blitz touchdowns. Fourth-down stops, coverage choices and costly pressure can each be recognized. This does
  not extend Third-Down Heat's gameplay trigger to fourth down or claim a habit caused an ordinary call. At most
  four facts per DC/game persist independently of replay retention; the recap still selects one staff observation
  within its existing three-sentence limit.
- Conversion mail can mention the destination teacher and a supported teaching tradeoff.

Message construction is read-only. Filed text remains a snapshot. Recommendations close after personnel/season
changes. A long simulation does not fabricate follow-up mail for a suggestion the user never received.

## Second wave and the fourth-down review — October 26, 2026

The first coach-quirks build failed CI's sim on field-goal attempts (2.002 against a 2.0 ceiling, main at
1.989). Matched games put the cause on the two habits that add kicks, Settles for Three and Shot-Play
Architect, held at 44% and 23% of offensive coordinators' chairs. The trades and head-coach-carousel failures
reproduced as world noise on main too.

The base engine was the other half. It went for it on fourth down 1.23 times a team-game against the NFL's
1.40–1.47 (2023–24), so its drives ended in kicks and punts. The fourth-down calls now go for it more on fourth
and short around midfield and on fourth and medium in opponent territory, head coaches' appetite is rolled
around +0.1 instead of 0, and the sim checks fourth-down tries against 1.3–1.6. To hold plays, and the season
leaders, where they were, a normal tempo's time between snaps went from 27–38 to 28–38 seconds.

Matched experiment, 2,000 games per habit, both teams carrying it (per team-game):

| Habit | Points Δ | FG attempts Δ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Goes for Two | −0.01 | −0.01 |
| Distrusts the Long Kick | −0.28 | −0.12 |
| Attacks the End Zone | +0.05 | −0.02 |
| Up-Tempo | +0.49 | +0.05 |
| Plays Prevent | +0.03 | 0.00 |
| Disguises Coverages | −0.12 | +0.02 |

30-season sim after the change: 22.14 points, 62.13 plays, 1.88 field-goal attempts and 1.43 fourth-down tries
a team-game, every league stat and season-leader row inside its range.

The trades check's two near misses are the market's own edges, not this work's. Over twelve leagues (36
seasons), in-season trades were 16.0 a season on main and 16.3 here. Deals voided or through with concerns
were 3.2 on main's default world but 4.3 and 3.8 in two other main worlds (band 1–4), so main sits at that
ceiling too. With every coaching piece off, this build plays main's games exactly. A three-league run can
land either number outside its band in any world; that calibration belongs to the trade market.
